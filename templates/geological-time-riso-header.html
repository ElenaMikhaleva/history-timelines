Geological Time

4,567 million years, four eons — a molten start and the thin final stretch that finally looks like somewhere.

History · Paleontology
4567 Mastart

Hadean

4567 – 4031 Ma

from Greek Hádēs, the underworld

Earth had just formed, and its surface was nothing like the one we know — unstable, and resurfaced constantly as the mantle pushed molten rock up from below. This eon ends the moment solid rock first appears in the record.

4031 Ma

Archean

4031 – 2500 Ma

from Greek arkhḗ, origin

  • The atmosphere was mostly methane and ammonia — unbreathable by nearly everything alive today
  • Rock and the first continents began forming at the start of this eon
  • A primitive atmosphere and early oceans took shape
  • Life itself appeared — the first bacteria
2500 Ma

Proterozoic

2500 – 538 Ma

from Greek próteros ('former') + zōḗ ('life')

  • Continents stabilized and grew, though nothing like today's map
  • Bacteria, fungi, simple plants, and the first animals evolved
  • Oxygen built up in the atmosphere for the first time
538 Ma→ today

Phanerozoic

538 – 0 Ma

from Greek phanerós ('visible') + zōḗ ('life')

Plants capable of photosynthesis appeared, releasing free oxygen at scale — and the world finally starts to look like somewhere. Just the last 12% of Earth's history, and everything visible happens here.

Three eras within the Phanerozoic

Paleozoic

538 – 252 Ma

Continents drifted and collided into one supercontinent, Pangaea.

  • Cambrian
  • Ordovician
  • Silurian
  • Devonian
  • Carboniferous
  • Permian

Mesozoic

252 – 66 Ma

The age of reptiles, until an asteroid impact ended the era.

  • Triassic
  • Jurassic
  • Cretaceous

Cenozoic

66 – 0 Ma

The age of mammals — and, at the very end, us.

  • Paleogene
  • Neogene
  • Quaternary
