Hadean Eon Катархей

4567 – 4031 Mya

from Greek Hádēs (underworld)

Precambrian Supereon.

Most of its history is reconstructed from indirect evidence, as very little evidence survived.

  • Earth forms and gradually cools;
  • Intense volcanic activity and frequent large asteroid impacts. Earth's surface is highly unstable, with widespread volcanism and crustal recycling;
  • Moon forms (giant impact hypothesis);
  • Eon ends with appearing of rock formation.

Archean Eon Архей

4031 – 2500 Mya

from Greek arkhḗ (beginning)

Precambrian Supereon.

Stabilization of early crust and onset of primitive life.

  • Atmosphere: methane, carbon dioxide, and nitrogene, no free molecular oxygen — unbreathable by nearly everything alive today:
    • methane contributed to the greenhouse effect and may have helped keep surface water liquid;
    • methane created ecosystem for methanotrophs — microbes that consumed it.
  • Continental crust and protocontinents formed;
  • Stromatolites appeared — solid, domed structures built of thin layers of sediment by colonial cyanobacteria;
  • First life appeared — prokaryotes (methanotrophs, cyanobacteria).

Proterozoic Eon Протерозой

2500 – 538 Mya

from Greek próteros (earlier) + zōḗ (life)

Precambrian Supereon.

Atmospheric oxidation and evolution of complex cells.

  • Continents stabilize and grow (Kenorland, Columbia, Rodinia, then Pannotia);
  • Atmosphere: oxygen accumulates for the first time, mainly because of photosynthesis by cyanobacteria;
  • Climate: global Ice Ages occur (Snowball Earth);
  • Sea: eukaryotes appear;
  • Sea: multi-cellular organisms appear (Ediacaran fauna), mostly soft-bodied with no traditional organs like mouth, guts.
  • Eon ends with the beginning of the Phanerozoic, when abundant animal fossils appear.

Phanerozoic Eon Фанерозой

538 Mya – today

from Greek phanerós (visible) + zōḗ (life)

Abundant complex life and hard shell/bone fossil record.

Paleozoic Era

 538 – 252 Mya

Early life diversification to Pangaea formation.

  • Cambrian (marine animals)
  • Ordovician
  • Silurian (land plants)
  • Devonian (fishes diversify)
  • Carboniferous (amphibians)
  • Permian (mass extinction)

Mesozoic Era

 252 – 66 Mya

The Age of Reptiles and breakup of Pangaea.

  • Triassic (dinosaurs & mammals)
  • Jurassic
  • Cretaceous (flowering plants)

Cenozoic Era

66 Mya – today

The Age of Mammals and recent Ice Ages.

  • Paleogene (mammals)
  • Neogene
  • Quaternary (humans)
