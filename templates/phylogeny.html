§ 01 — CLADE

Amphibia

diverged c. 350 Ma

ETMY: Greek amphíbios (living a double life)

The first tetrapods to fully colonize land while retaining an aquatic larval stage. Frogs, toads, salamanders, and caecilians all descend from this lineage, which split from the amniote line before amniotes existed at all — the earliest branch on this tree.

§ 02 — CLADE

Synapsida → Mammalia

diverged c. 320 Ma

ETMY: Greek syn- (together) + hapsis (arch)

The lineage that eventually produced mammals split from the reptile line roughly 320 million years ago — long before anything resembling a modern mammal existed. Fur, live birth, and a single jaw-hinge bone all evolved gradually within this branch over the following 300+ million years.

§ 03 — CLADE

Sauropsida → Reptilia

split c. 260 Ma

ETMY: Greek saûros (lizard) + ópsis (face)

The reptile line itself split roughly 260 million years ago into two branches that still look almost nothing alike today, despite sharing this one ancestor.

Archosauria → Crocodiles

also the lineage of birds

Crocodilians are the closest living relatives of birds — both descend from the archosaur branch of this split.

Lepidosauria → Snakes

also lizards, tuatara

Snakes are limbless squamates that evolved within Lepidosauria, the sister lineage to archosaurs on this tree.
