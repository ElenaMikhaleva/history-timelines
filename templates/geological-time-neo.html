Hadean Катархей

4567 – 4031 Ma

from Greek Hádēs (the underworld) + aiṓn ('life force')

Earth had just formed, and its surface was nothing like the one we know — unstable, and resurfaced constantly as the mantle pushed molten rock up from below. This eon ends the moment solid rock first appears in the record.

Archean Архей

4031 – 2500 Ma

from Greek arkhḗ ('origin')

  • The atmosphere was mostly methane and ammonia — unbreathable by nearly everything alive today
  • Rock and the first continents began forming at the start of this eon
  • A primitive atmosphere and early oceans took shape
  • Life itself appeared — the first bacteria

Proterozoic Протерозой

2500 – 538 Ma

from Greek próteros ('former') + zōḗ ('life')

  • Continents stabilized and grew, though they looked nothing like today's map
  • Bacteria, fungi, simple plants, and the first animals evolved
  • Oxygen built up in the atmosphere for the first time

Phanerozoic Фанерозой

538 – 0 Ma

from Greek phanerós ('visible') + zōḗ ('life') — named before the Precambrian fossil record was known, when life seemed to begin here

Plants capable of photosynthesis appeared, releasing free oxygen into the atmosphere at scale for the first time — and setting up almost everything that follows.

Paleozoic

538 – 252 Ma

Continents drifted and eventually collided into one supercontinent, Pangaea.

Mesozoic

252 – 66 Ma

The age of reptiles. Pangaea broke apart as dinosaurs rose to dominate land, sea, and air — until an asteroid impact ended the era.

  • Triassic
  • Jurassic
  • Cretaceous

Cenozoic

66 – 0 Ma

The age of mammals. In its final, brief stretch, one species — humans — began writing all of this down.
