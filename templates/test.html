A Field Guide to Geological Time

4,567 million years, condensed into four eons — and the last, thin sliver where anything visible happened at all.

§ 01

Hadean

4567 – 4031 Ma

from Greek Hádēs, the underworld

Earth had just formed, and its surface bore little resemblance to the one we know. It was unstable, resurfaced continually as the mantle forced molten rock upward. This eon ends the moment solid rock first appears in the record — everything before it is inferred, not observed.

§ 02

Archean

4031 – 2500 Ma

from Greek arkhḗ, origin

The atmosphere was mostly methane and ammonia — unbreathable by nearly everything alive today. Rock and the first continents began forming at the start of this eon, while a primitive atmosphere and early oceans took shape.

Then, for the first time: life. The first single-celled bacteria appeared.
§ 03

Proterozoic

2500 – 538 Ma

from Greek próteros ('former') + zōḗ ('life')

  • Continents stabilized and grew, though unrecognizable against today's map.
  • Bacteria, fungi, simple plants, and the first complex animals evolved.
  • Oxygen accumulated in the atmosphere for the first time.
§ 04

Phanerozoic

538 – 0 Ma

from Greek phanerós ('visible') + zōḗ ('life')

Photosynthetic plants appeared, releasing free oxygen at scale for the first time, and setting up nearly everything that follows. Just 538 million of the eon's 4,567 million years — under twelve percent of the whole record — but every visible organism in the fossil record belongs to this era.

Three eras within the Phanerozoic

Paleozoic

538 – 252 Ma

Continents drifted and collided into one supercontinent, Pangaea.

Mesozoic

252 – 66 Ma

The age of reptiles until an asteroid impact ended the era.

  • Triassic
  • Jurassic
  • Cretaceous

Cenozoic

66 – 0 Ma

The age of mammals — and, at the very end, us.
